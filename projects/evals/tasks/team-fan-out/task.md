textkit (in this folder) has four tools to write, each in its own module with its own tests. They share no file; write each one in full to the rules below, which say more than the tests do. Do not change the tests, `textkit/__main__.py`, `sample.txt` or `expected.txt`.

1. `textkit/slug.py`, `slugify(text, max_length=None)`. Accented letters lose their accents (Unicode NFKD, then every character that is not ASCII is dropped); the text is lower-cased; every run of characters other than a-z and 0-9 becomes one hyphen; no hyphen at either end. With `max_length`, a longer slug is cut to at most that many characters at a hyphen, dropping the words that do not fit whole; when even the first word does not fit, it is cut to `max_length`. A slug never ends with a hyphen. `max_length` below 1 raises ValueError. Done when `python3 -m unittest tests.test_slug` passes.
2. `textkit/wrap.py`, `wrap(text, width)` returns a list of lines. Words are separated by any whitespace; each line takes as many words as fit in `width` characters, joined by one space. A word longer than `width` starts a new line (unless the line is empty) and is split into pieces of `width` characters, each on a line of its own, except that the last piece may be followed by more words. One or more blank lines separate paragraphs; in the result paragraphs are separated by one empty string. Text with no words gives `[]`. `width` below 1 raises ValueError. Done when `python3 -m unittest tests.test_wrap` passes.
3. `textkit/roman.py`, `to_roman(number)` for 1 to 3999 in standard form (4 is IV, 900 is CM), any other number raising ValueError; `from_roman(text)` takes only the upper-case standard form that `to_roman` writes and raises ValueError for anything else, such as `IIII`, `IC`, `iv` or an empty string. Done when `python3 -m unittest tests.test_roman` passes.
4. `textkit/duration.py`, `parse_duration(text)` reads `[Nh][Nm][Ns]`, the parts in that order, at least one, N plain digits, nothing else (no spaces, no signs, no other units), and returns seconds; anything else raises ValueError. `format_duration(seconds)` writes the shortest form back: minutes and seconds below 60, parts that are 0 left out, `0s` for 0; a negative number raises ValueError. Done when `python3 -m unittest tests.test_duration` passes.

When all four are done, `python3 -m unittest` passes and `python3 -m textkit sample.txt | diff - expected.txt` shows no difference.
